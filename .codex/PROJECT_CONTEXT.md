# Project context

- Engine: Godot 4.7.
- Platform: Android; developed on Android.
- Format: 2D idle game.
- Theme: managing an ant colony.
- Core objective: “big number goes bigger.”
- Main-game visuals use `Polygon2D` nodes and particles, with role-specific transparent pixel-art sprites for queens, workers, and defenders.
- Other imported artwork remains reserved for GUI elements; ant sprites are stored as transparent `Queen.png`, `Worker.png`, and `Warrior.png` files in `assets/Ants`, converted from the corresponding JPEG sources.

## Version menu

- `scenes/main_menu.tscn` is the startup scene configured in `project.godot`. It uses the framed pixel-art menu and button textures from `assets/GUI/FreeDemo.png`.
- Play opens `MainScene.tscn`; Reset clears the saved colony and restores starting values; Exit quits the game.
- `scenes/queen_ant_1.tscn` remains a separate playable variant using the existing food-source foraging loop.
- Both game scenes can return to the main menu through their in-game Main Menu buttons.
- GUI buttons in the main menu and both playable scenes use the pixel-art button slices from `assets/GUI/FreeDemo.png` for normal, hover, and pressed states. Food and live-ant counter labels sit on matching nine-sliced atlas panels. Food and other world visuals remain polygons and particles; queens, workers, and defenders use their transparent role-specific ant sprites.

## Food amount

- The global `GameState` autoload in `scripts/game_state.gd` owns `food_amount`, initialized to 250 and clamped so it cannot fall below zero.
- Change the amount through `GameState.add_food(amount)` and `GameState.spend_food(amount)`. Adding ignores non-positive values; spending returns `false` when the requested amount is negative or unavailable, and otherwise deducts it and returns `true`.
- `GameState.food_changed(amount)` is emitted when the amount changes. UI that displays food should listen to this signal and also read `GameState.food_amount` when it starts.
- Both playable scenes keep the food and live-ant counters below the top utility buttons. Recenter and Main Menu stay at the top; purchase actions sit in a full-width, bottom-anchored vertical scroll area sized to show four buttons at a time on portrait touch screens. A full-width button above that list toggles between unit purchases and upgrades; its label names the currently hidden list. Purchase buttons and their VBox pass touch drags to the scroll container; camera pan and zoom input is ignored over the area. Ants and food use polygons; workers are 2 by 3 AntSource-local units, rendered at four times that size because the shared AntSource world root is scaled 4x. All game objects and world spacing under AntSource scale together; CanvasLayer GUI elements remain unchanged. Coordinated cluster gathering and delivery, and automatic queen food production are implemented.
- Workers are divided as evenly as possible among the available queen-led groups in both variants, with group sizes differing by at most one. A single queen means one group contains all workers; there is no per-group worker cap. Group members gather independently, then follow one another back to the stockpile after every member has finished gathering. Each ant deposits food upon reaching the stockpile, then settles at a scattered nearby position and wanders locally while waiting for its group; the leader searches again after all arrive. Incremental 1 worker purchases start at 10 food and grow by 1.02x; Queen Ant 1 direct worker purchases start at 5 and grow by 1.25x.
- Queen Ant 1 queens cost 300 food initially and each next queen price grows by 1.45x. Each queen spawns one worker every five seconds. The queen production upgrade raises the worker spawn rate, with each level shortening the spawn interval; its cost grows by 1.3x. Worker purchases start at 5 food and grow by 1.25x. Incremental 1 does not directly buy queens for passive food; queens unlock additional active worker groups.
- Defenders in both variants cost a constant 100 food each and unlock for purchase after the colony has four queens. They patrol around grid points that have reached 100 steps and only engage worms within 12 pixels of their assigned saturated point; with no saturated points they hold position and do not attack. Worms still cross the 120-pixel colony radius on a randomized chord that may miss the center and despawn after crossing the far edge; their 12–20 second spawn delay is unchanged. Worms have 24 health, and each defender attack deals 4 base damage plus Warrior Damage levels while costing the defender 1 health. Defeating one drops six harvestable red food polygons worth 60 food each. Defender count and health persist in saves; loaded defenders are retained even if the colony has fewer than four queens.
- Warrior Damage (+1 per attack, max 4), Warrior Endurance (+2 max and current health, max 5), and Warrior Training (+10% movement speed multiplicatively, max 5) are available after four queens in both variants. Their first costs are 250, 400, and 600 food, with respective cost multipliers of 1.5, 1.6, and 1.7. Levels and warrior health persist; details are in `.codex/ant_source_behavior_and_plan.md`.
- Queen Ant 1 carry adds one food per worker trip per level. Speed increases by 10% per level. Food efficiency increases generated food-source stockpiles by 25% per level for future sources. Core Queen Ant 1 upgrade prices start at 25 food.
- Incremental 1 purchases are documented in `.codex/incremental_1_unlockables.md`. Worker gathering-time upgrades unlock after the first speed level, cost 250 food initially, reduce the base gathering pause by 10% multiplicatively per level, and cap at six levels. Queens unlock at 25 workers, cost 500 food initially, and remain capped at 10 queens. Carry, speed, source-size, queen, source-amount, and gathering-time cost curves are documented alongside their unlocks. Source-size upgrades add 25 food to each live source and future sources benefit from the source-size level.
- Queen Ant 1 carry, speed, and worker-lifetime upgrades start at 25 food and grow by 1.15x per purchase. Queen production and food-efficiency upgrades start at 25 and grow by 1.3x. The text-only purchase UI abbreviates large displayed values with K, M, and B.
- Queen Ant 1 keeps the same food-foraging gameplay, but queens spawn one worker per five seconds each instead of generating food directly. Its queen production upgrade increases worker spawn rate; workers expire after 30 seconds, and each worker-lifetime upgrade level adds 10 seconds. Direct worker purchases are also available.
- Every Queen Ant 1 worker spawns in the queen's deposit square and joins the smallest eligible leader group. Expired workers disappear. A worker that dies while carrying food leaves a harvestable, 1 by 1 pixel food drop at its death position; leaders do not target these drops.
- `GameState` loads and saves a local JSON snapshot at `user://game_state.json`. Successful purchases save immediately; active games autosave every 10 seconds, save when Android pauses the app or the window closes, and establish a save timestamp on scene entry. Incremental 1 grants offline queen food production for up to 24 hours using the saved queen count and production level; Queen Ant 1 grants no offline food because its queens spawn temporary workers rather than producing food directly. Older snapshots default new economy fields to their initial values and are treated as Incremental 1 saves for offline earnings.
- Step-grid tracking is capability-based: ants with `AntParticle.can_dig` enabled register visits on a triangular grid with 15 px edges centered at the queen. Currently only workers are created with this capability; queens and defenders do not dig, and future ant types can opt in through the same flag. All ants move more slowly near vertices below 100 steps: their speed multiplier is `1 - 0.75 * (1 - steps / 100)`, giving 25% speed at 0 steps and full speed at 100. Each worker-group leader routes across the grid using weighted shortest paths: each edge costs distance divided by its average movement-speed multiplier, so well-stepped terrain is preferred when it reduces travel time. Followers follow the ant ahead of them along the leader's route without calculating their own route. With no visited points, leaders continue directly to targets. A step is registered when a dig-capable ant's movement segment passes within 7 px of a grid point, once per ant until it leaves that point's radius; counts cap at 100 and persist in `user://game_state.json`. Worker Dig Speed unlocks after the first Speed upgrade in each mode, costs 300 food initially with 1.5x price growth, and adds one step per pass per level on top of the base one; its level and next cost persist across both modes. Each visited point's collider-active state is saved too; its collider disables at 100 steps and stays disabled after loading. New food sources avoid saturated points by the point collider radius plus the source radius and 2 px clearance. The initial grid covers the colony radius and expands by neighboring points when a dig-capable ant reaches its edge. The `Heat Map: Off/On` GUI button in either game scene toggles the visited-point heat map from blue (0) through the hue spectrum to red (100), drawn above world objects and below the CanvasLayer GUI.
- Worker movement also paints a persistent trail: a 5 px radius brush lays opaque gray marks on a 1-unit paint grid, sampled every 2.5 world units, whose shade follows the nearby step heat-map count, with higher heat becoming lighter gray. At the 4x world scale, each cell renders at about 4 screen pixels. Trail cells are stored and drawn in 32 by 32 chunks; heat shading is calculated only for new cells, and only affected chunks redraw. The exact grayscale shade is stored per painted cell in `user://game_state.json`, restored in both playable modes, and older trail formats are migrated on load. Trail rendering settings do not change movement or step-count calculations. Render order is trail, food polygons, queens, warriors, then workers. The trail uses the minimum supported CanvasItem z index, food polygons use the raised `FoodSources` container z index, and ant z indexes are assigned by type. The optional `Heat Map` visited-point debug overlay remains above the world.

## Ant spawning and foraging

- `AntSource` is a direct child of each game scene, is scaled 4x, and centers its world origin on the viewport at startup. The shared camera's startup/recenter action centers on the live ants' center of mass and chooses a zoom that fits the farthest ant with 48 viewport pixels of padding, capped at 10x. Manual panning and zooming remain available after framing. CanvasLayer GUI elements are outside the scaled world and retain their configured size.
- A loaded colony always has at least one queen, including after recovering a save with a zero queen count.
- Workers spawn in the queen-centered 10 by 10 pixel deposit square and are assigned to the smallest eligible leader group; ties favor the nearest leader. The number of active groups is limited by the queen count, and group sizes are balanced to differ by at most one as workers or queens are added and workers expire. Leaders search live regular food polygons from nearest to farthest, skipping sources another group is traveling to or gathering from; if no unclaimed source remains, they scout. All group ants travel to the selected source and each ant begins scattering within the source radius plus 1 pixel as soon as it arrives, without waiting for the rest of the group. Each ant gathers independently, but the group waits until every member's gather timer ends, then followers keep following the ant ahead of them on the trip back. Each ant deposits its carried food immediately upon reaching its stockpile position. Ants then spread to scattered nearby positions and wander locally while waiting for the whole group to arrive; after everyone arrives, the leader searches again and its group follows. If a target polygon depletes during outbound travel, affected ants regroup with the smallest eligible leader group regardless of distance or form a new group. If it depletes while they are gathering, ants first look for the smallest eligible leader group; those without one return to the deposit square and deliver carried food, then look again. Death drops are not forage targets. If no polygon exists, leaders scout within `food_search_range` of the queen's spawn point.
- Any worker that becomes idle joins the smallest eligible leader group, with nearest leader breaking ties; without one, it forms a group and resumes searching.
- If a Queen Ant 1 worker expires while carrying food, its carried amount becomes a harvestable 1 by 1 pixel food drop at the worker's death position. Expired ants disappear.
- Each regular food polygon depletion in either variant rolls a 1-in-10 chance to create tappable super food at its position. It is a bright yellow oval bug at 0 degrees, distinct from the orange food polygons, with glitter particles that scale with it. Its screen-space size follows camera zoom, with a 30-pixel minimum. It stays hidden and untappable until its initial camera-scaled size is set, then disappears 50 seconds after spawning. It renders above the ants but below the CanvasLayer UI. Ants cannot collect it. Tapping it doubles worker movement speed for 10 seconds; collecting another during the boost refreshes the timer.
